# What we're focusing on, together

**A focus node is what the human is focusing on, with the model: an intention, plus the memory of pursuing it.** Focus is co-created. The sphere has no focus of its own, apart from the human's.

A sphere holds hundreds of files: work, knowledge, lenses. *"What's in focus right now?"* needs a clear answer, and a focus node is that answer. It also ties the sphere together in one sentence: *focus X, for work Y, using knowledge Z, through lens W.*

## What a focus keeps, and what it borrows

- **It keeps its intention** (why it exists) **and its memory** (what was done, decided and learned while pursuing it). That's exactly what a good ground writes down, and exactly what survives a window closing.
- **It doesn't keep context.** Context is built fresh each time something attaches, from the intention, the memory and what the focus holds. It fills the window and is gone when the window closes.
- **It borrows ability.** What the AI knows how to do lives in lenses and frames. A focus borrows it through what it works through and the operating mode it's in.

## Sessions and agents are two ways of working a focus

A window, the app or terminal or run attached right now, isn't the focus. It attaches, works, and detaches; the focus lives on. How a window works on a focus depends on **who chooses each step:**

| | Who chooses each step | The intention | Collaboration mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Session** | the human, turn by turn | drifts with each turn, taking on whatever the turn needs | together |
| **Agent** | the model, with the human checking at gates or afterwards | usually fixed, set by the focus as its starter | supervised or unattended |

The first column is what makes the difference. A fixed intention is common for agents, but it isn't what makes one.

- **Memory belongs to the focus, not to whatever works it.** An agent elsewhere keeps its memory in its context window, or in its vendor's store. In a sphere it's the focus node's history, in files the human can read. Anything that attaches gets the same memory.
- **Delegating is opening a child focus.** The child's intention is the agent's task; it reports back in one line.
- **Who, what, and how stay separate.** A persona is *who* the AI is. A focus is *what* the human is pursuing. A collaboration mode is *who chooses each step*. An agent's definition (what it may do, how far it's trusted) is a fourth thing. A sphere keeps all of these apart, and keeps the memory with the human.

## In hand, and in mind

Every open focus is something the sphere is holding for the human. **Foreground** is open with something attached: in view right now. **Background** is open with nothing attached: not in view, but held. That's how a person works: one thing in hand, several in mind.

- **Foreground means the model is attending, not that the human is.** An agent running unattended is in the foreground.
- **Only closing ends a focus.** Detaching sends it to the background; nothing is forgotten.

## Focus is not work

Work is what's changing in the world. Focus is what's being attended to. Many foci hold exactly one piece of work, but a focus can hold several, or only knowledge (*"read up on heat pumps"*), or the sphere itself. **Work is the line; foci are the dots** along it, each picking some of it up and putting it down. A piece of work is *in focus* when an open focus holds it.

## Narrowing, and knowing when to stop

**Moving down the focus tree narrows the focus.** The root is main (`f0-main`), the sphere itself: always open, and where a quick window grounds without opening anything. Its children are the broad things in view (the kitchen, this week); theirs are specific pursuits (three plumbing quotes).

**When the intention drifts, the shape says what to do.** A little drift is a rename. Drifting out of the node, with the old intention unfinished, is a child. Drifting out with it finished is a close, and a new focus. The sphere notices and asks; the human decides. **Closing stops being a matter of feel,** or of the window wearing out.

**Depth is a dial.** A sphere that never opens a child has foci side by side, one level deep. The tree only grows when someone narrows a focus or delegates.

## How it shows up

- **Talk about focus is a command.** *"Open a focus for the kitchen"* opens one; *"continue that session"* attaches. It's never read as "I'm about to work on the kitchen."
- **Every change to a focus is reported in one line,** so a miss shows on the next turn.
- **A focus writes only what it holds.** Anything else is appended, offered, proposed or rebuilt. That one rule is what lets several foci, sessions and agents share one sphere on one branch.
- **Nodes talk through their parent, never sideways.**

## What it doesn't mean

- **Not every window is a focus.** A quick question that never grounds leaves nothing behind.
- **Not a to-do list.** Focus holds attention, not tasks. Tasks are work.
- **Not the sphere's agenda.** The sphere may suggest a focus from signals (a deadline, the week's plan). It never opens one on its own say-so.
