# Growing by crystallization

**A sphere is never finished.** It's shaped by how its human actually works, and that keeps changing. So the sphere changes too: a pattern that keeps coming up gets a name, a frame that's grown awkward gets reshaped, a near-miss becomes a rule. **It changes by crystallization:** something already happening in use is noticed, named and written down, so from then on it happens on purpose.

**Every change is co-authored, and only on the human's instruction.** The sphere notices and proposes. The human decides, and brings the understanding in their own words. The AI drafts, the human corrects, and the two iterate until it's true to how they work. Nothing about how the sphere understands its human, or how it behaves, changes on the AI's own initiative.

## One loop

```
use → notice → propose → (the human decides) → crystallize → announce → use
                  ↑
               reflect
```

- **Use is where everything starts.** A capability isn't designed in the abstract. It's lifted out of what the human and the AI were already doing.
- **Reflection balances growth.** Adding is easy; a sphere that only adds fills up with things nobody uses. Reflecting is how it notices what's missing, what's unused and what no longer fits, and shrinks where it should.
- **Announcing teaches.** When something lands, the sphere says what changed in the human's terms. That's how the vocabulary of the sphere is learned: from real examples, not documentation.

## Size the change first

**Most things don't deserve a structural change.** The first question is always how big this really is: a one-off that's just done, a new way to ask for something, a new ability in an existing frame, a new frame, or, rarely, a new lens. **Prefer updating to adding, at every level.** A new lens needs a *why* no existing lens holds; mechanics alone earn a frame, never a lens.

**How a change is made follows from how much it touches.** A small one is made in one sitting. A large one is built beside the old structure, which keeps working, and switched over in one declared step. A fundamental one is built as a new sphere alongside the old. **The sphere is never left half-changed between grounds.**

## Rules come from real moments

**A guardrail is born from a specific moment:** something nearly went wrong, or the human corrected the AI. The sphere offers a rule while the moment is fresh, and **the rule carries its why.** *Why the why:* a rule without its reason can't be applied well to a case nobody foresaw, and can't be retired safely when the reason goes away.

**Rules are earned, not aspirational.** A rule written because it sounds wise competes with the ones that matter. And when the AI makes a non-obvious call and the human says *"yes, exactly"*, that's worth keeping too. A sphere that only records corrections slowly grows timid.

## The sphere's own maybes

**What the sphere notices but hasn't acted on lives as ideas about the sphere:** [idea](idea-capture.md) files with the `sphere` topic. A lens to sharpen, a frame that's awkward, a check that's missing. Each time evolution comes up, that collection is reviewed: each idea is done, graduates into a proposed change, or is parked. Nothing is lost, and nothing is acted on without a yes.

## The checks change with the sphere

**Every change to the sphere's shape updates the checks that guard it, in the same step.** A new tree, layer or kind of file, or a changed frontmatter, updates `core/shapes` and the [structure check](../frames/readable-substrate/structure-check.md) together. The registries that list what changed are rebuilt in the same ground. *Why:* a check that lags the sphere reports noise, and a check that reports noise gets ignored.

## How it shows up

- **Asked, never assumed.** The AI may notice a repeated pattern and ask whether to capture it. It never writes a lens, a frame or a rule because it seemed like a good idea.
- **Outside sphere authoring, the sphere's internals are read-only.** A change it notices is captured as a `sphere` idea and left for the next reflection. See [sphere authoring](../../../../spec/modes.md#sphere-authoring-changing-the-sphere-itself--decided-not-yet-exercised).
- **A rhythm, not a schedule.** A sphere tends to need evolution every couple of weeks. The sphere recognizes the signal itself (a concept with nowhere to live, a frame that feels stretched, friction with something that used to work) and suggests a reflection at a good moment.
- **Plain words for a non-technical human.** They never hear *lens* or *frame*. *"Done. Just say 'how was my week' any time."*
- **The story stays home.** A lens or frame keeps a short changelog of what changed. Why it changed (the incident, the people, the dates) lives in the sphere's private records, so what's learned can travel without the story behind it.

## What it doesn't mean

- **Not growth for its own sake.** A sphere that adds freely becomes hard to read and starts contradicting itself. Removing something is as much evolution as adding it.
- **Not the AI improving itself.** The AI can see what would help. The human decides what the sphere becomes.
- **Not a migration project for every change.** Most changes are a line in an existing frame, made in a minute.
- **Not moving capabilities between spheres.** That's [replication](replication.md). Evolution changes this sphere, in place.
